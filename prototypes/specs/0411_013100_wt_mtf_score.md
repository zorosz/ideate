# WaveTrend Multi-Timeframe Score

## Thesis

The WaveTrend crossover edge is validated on M1 (PF 1.09, #1 in
IDEAS.md). The M10 confirmation filter improved every metric and is
named the "most valuable filter" (Key Learning #1). The natural
extension: if ONE higher timeframe confirmation helps, do MORE
confirmations help even more?

The hypothesis is monotonic: each additional aligned timeframe
represents another participant cohort. M1 = scalpers and HFT, M5 = day
traders, M10 = intraday swing traders, M15 = retail systems and short-
term institutional. When all four agree on the direction of WT
exhaustion, the trade has the maximum number of aligned participants.

This is the most direct attempt to improve the validated edge. The risk
is well-understood: more filters = fewer trades = potential overfitting
(see #5: BOS direction filter failed for exactly this reason). The
question is whether MTF alignment is "fewer better trades" or "fewer
trades, same edge per trade" or "fewer trades, worse per trade." Only
backtesting will tell.

## Evidence

1. **WT crossover with M10 confirmation validated (#1, PF 1.09)**: this
   is the baseline. The edge is real, the trade count is healthy (958
   trades over 2 years), DD is 0.44%. Anything we test must beat this.

2. **Key Learning #1 — M10 WT confirmation is the most valuable
   filter**: explicitly named by the project. The principle is "higher
   TF agreement matters." This strategy generalizes the principle to
   multiple TFs.

3. **DECISION 2026-04-05 — Do NOT add RSI**: the principle is "don't
   pile redundant filters." MTF alignment uses the SAME indicator on
   different timeframes. The question is whether different TFs are
   "redundant" (same WT, just slower) or "complementary" (different
   participant cohorts). This is the empirical test.

4. **BOS direction filter failed (#5)**: ~9 trades over a year. Filter
   was too restrictive on M1. The lesson: any new filter must not
   collapse trade count below ~100/year. This sets the boundary for
   v1 (most restrictive).

5. **WT divergence failed (#6)**: WT-based extensions have failed
   before. Pure divergence patterns are noise. MTF alignment is
   structurally different (it's filter, not signal generation), but
   serves as a cautionary tale.

6. **Fama/French multi-factor literature**: in equities, multi-factor
   models work better than single factors. The analogue here is multi-
   timeframe filtering as a "factor stack." Theoretical support exists.

## Base (v1)

**Timeframe**: M1 entries, M5/M10/M15 confirmations

**Setup detection** (every M1 bar):
- Compute WaveTrend on M1, M5, M10, M15
- Track WT cross on M1: bullish cross (`wt1` crosses above `wt2`) or
  bearish cross (`wt1` crosses below `wt2`)
- Compute alignment score:
  - For BUY: score = (M5 oversold? 1 : 0) + (M10 oversold? 1 : 0) +
              (M15 oversold? 1 : 0)
  - For SELL: score = (M5 overbought? 1 : 0) + (M10 overbought? 1 : 0) +
               (M15 overbought? 1 : 0)
- Trade only if score == 3 (full alignment)

**Entry**:
- Market entry on M1 cross bar close, only if score == 3
- Single position, never both directions
- One-position-per-direction max

**Exit**:
- TP: ATR(14, M1) × `TP_ATR` (default 2.5)
- SL: ATR(14, M1) × `SL_ATR` (default 1.5)
- Trail to break-even after price moves ATR × `TrailTrigger` in profit

**Indicators**: WaveTrend on M1, M5, M10, M15; ATR(14, M1)

## v2: Score >= 2 of 3 (looser)

Same as v1 but require alignment score >= 2 (at least 2 of 3 higher TFs
agree), not the full 3.

**Why this might work**: Sweet spot between WT-alone and full alignment.
Captures more trades than v1 while still adding meaningful filtering.
May be the version that beats the BOS-filter overfit.

**Why this might fail**: Loosening the filter may revert performance to
WT-alone levels. The "score" becomes informational but not selective.

## v3: Score-weighted lot sizing

Same as v1 but vary position size by alignment score:
- Score 0 (only M1 cross): no trade
- Score 1: lot = 0.01
- Score 2: lot = 0.02
- Score 3: lot = 0.03
- All trades use the same SL/TP rules

**Why this might work**: Confidence-based sizing is the optimal Kelly-
style approach. Higher conviction = larger position. Captures more
trades than v1 while adding sizing edge.

**Why this might fail**: Larger positions on rare full-alignment days
concentrates risk. If the rare-alignment trade is the regime breaker,
the larger position doubles the loss.

## v4: Sequential cross sequence

Same as v1 but require WT crosses to occur in sequence: M1 cross →
M5 cross within `MaxLagBars` bars → M10 cross within `MaxLagBars` bars
of M5.

**Why this might work**: Sequential cross is a stronger structural
event than static OS/OB alignment. It captures the "wave" of momentum
shift across timeframes — what TA literature calls "cascading
reversals."

**Why this might fail**: M1 crosses are common, M5/M10 crosses are
rare. Sequential filter may collapse to single-digit trades per year.
Same overfitting risk as BOS.

## v5: Tighter TF stack (M1 + M5 + M10 only)

Same as v1 but drop M15 from the stack. Score is M5 + M10 (max 2).
Require score == 2 (both higher TFs aligned).

**Why this might work**: M15 may be too slow for M1 entries. The
alignment between M1 and M15 is rarely meaningful for short-term
trades. Removing it may improve signal quality without losing
information.

**Why this might fail**: M15 is the structurally meaningful TF (it
captures longer cohorts). Dropping it may reduce edge.

## v6: Looser stack (M1 + M15 only)

Same as v1 but drop M5 and M10. Use only M1 + M15 (max score 1).
Require M15 OS/OB on M1 cross.

**Why this might work**: M15 is the highest-impact TF in the stack.
Maybe just M1 + M15 captures most of the edge with the highest signal
frequency.

**Why this might fail**: Single-TF filter is what M10 confirmation
already does (validated baseline). May not improve over the M10
version. Or may be slightly worse because M15 is noisier than M10.

## v7: ATR-adjusted TP/SL by score

Same as v1 but TP/SL scales with alignment score:
- Score 1: tight (TP=1.5*ATR, SL=1*ATR)
- Score 2: medium (TP=2.5*ATR, SL=1.5*ATR)
- Score 3: wide (TP=3.5*ATR, SL=2*ATR)

Higher alignment = more conviction = wider stops to allow the trade
to develop.

**Why this might work**: High-conviction trades deserve room to breathe.
Low-conviction trades should exit quickly.

**Why this might fail**: Adds parameters that may overfit. The simpler
approach (uniform stops) is more robust.

## Notes

**Market condition targeted**: Same as WT baseline — mean-reverting
periods with momentum exhaustion. Filter explicitly avoids low-
confidence setups.

**Risk profile**: Mid win rate (target 50-55%, +5-10% over baseline),
modest R:R (target 1.5-2:1, similar to baseline). Improvement should
come from higher WR, not bigger winners.

**Critical comparison metrics** (vs WT-alone baseline):
- Trade count: must be > 50/year (not collapsed like BOS filter)
- PF improvement: must be > 5% (not just noise)
- Sharpe improvement: should track PF improvement
- Max DD: should be lower (filter removes bad trades)

**Implementation note**: M5/M10/M15 WaveTrend should be computed using
COMPLETED bars only — never the in-progress higher-TF bar. Use
`iBars(_Symbol, PERIOD_M5)` and read from index 1 (last completed).
Look-ahead bias is a major risk for MTF strategies.

**Edge cases**:
1. **First bar of higher TF**: when M5 just rolls over, the M5 WT value
   is stale (computed on prior bar). Acceptable as long as it's
   consistent.
2. **TF disagreement around levels**: M1 crosses oversold but M5 is
   neutral. Strategy correctly skips.
3. **Multiple M1 crosses inside one M5 bar**: each M1 cross gets
   evaluated against the same M5 state. Results in clusters of
   identical-quality trades.

**Failure modes to watch**:
- v1 trades < 50/year → too restrictive, likely overfitting like BOS
- v1 PF < 1.10 → MTF filter adds nothing over WT-alone
- v1 PF > 1.30 → suspicious; check for look-ahead bias on higher TFs
- v3 lot scaling produces erratic equity curve → confidence sizing is
  not edge, just noise amplification

**Open question for prototype phase**: which version best balances
trade count vs quality? Hypothesis ranking (most likely to succeed):
v2 > v5 > v1 > v6 > v3 > v4 > v7. Test in this order.
